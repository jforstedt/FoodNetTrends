# Monthly coverage evidence: Salmonella and Campylobacter, 2004–2019

Reviewed 2026-09-13. **Public evidence supports an ongoing active surveillance program and a stable geographic catchment; it does not independently certify every site–pathogen–month in our extract.** This review supports monthly preparation, not model fitting or automatic zero filling.

## What the primary sources establish

| Evidence | What it supports | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| The final 2004 CDC report describes active surveillance in all ten sites. Personnel contacted each of 604 clinical laboratories weekly or monthly, depending on laboratory size. It also presents January–December seasonal distributions for both pathogens. | Repeated laboratory ascertainment and an established monthly descriptive use of the data. | A complete laboratory-contact ledger or interruption calendar for every site/month. |
| A CDC-authored analysis of FoodNet cases during 2004–2011 states that the catchment was stable from 2004. | Using the historical catchment as a starting geographic frame. | Constant ascertainment, test availability, or laboratory participation within that frame. |
| New Mexico's health department explicitly dates its active FoodNet surveillance start to January 1, 2004. | January 2004 is a documented start boundary for New Mexico. | Retrospective confirmation of uninterrupted service in each subsequent month. |
| CDC's 2019 report includes both pathogens across the ten-site surveillance area and discusses culture-independent testing. | Continued inclusion at the period's endpoint and the need to distinguish testing changes from incidence changes. | A month-specific completeness guarantee for the entire 2004–2019 interval. |

Sources: [CDC final 2004 report, methods and seasonality, printed pages 5–7](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/22315/cdc_22315_DS1.pdf); [CDC-authored 2004–2011 analysis, methods](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/22/7/15-0833_article); [New Mexico Department of Health FoodNet program](https://www.nmhealth.org/about/erd/ideb/eip/foodnet/); [CDC 2019 surveillance report](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/69/wr/mm6917a1.htm).

CDC's current program description corroborates routine laboratory contact and periodic reporting audits. Its public data guide includes monthly percentages but limits the tool to reported laboratory-confirmed infections. Neither description claims complete ascertainment of all community infections. These contemporary descriptions support interpretation of the system; they cannot substitute for historical operational records. [Program methods](https://www.cdc.gov/foodnet/index.html), [FoodNet Fast guide](https://www.cdc.gov/foodnet/data/pathogen-surveillance.html).

## Coverage still unresolved

The proposed interval contains 192 calendar months. For each of CA, CO, CT, GA, MD, MN, NM, NY, OR and TN, and separately for Salmonella and Campylobacter, **monthly observation eligibility remains unconfirmed by this public-source review**. The sources inspected did not supply a site-month interruption ledger. That is absence of documentation found, not evidence that interruptions occurred or that none occurred. State labels refer to FoodNet catchments; CA, CO and NY are not whole-state populations in this period.

A nonzero case count proves that at least one case was recorded, not complete reporting. An empty month could represent zero qualifying cases, missing ascertainment, incomplete transfer, or a date/filter issue. Annual totals and valid annual population denominators do not distinguish these possibilities.

Before zero filling, obtain a program or site-custodian statement covering the interval, with exceptions for start/stop dates, reporting gaps, backfilled records, and laboratory service changes. Translate that evidence into a versioned site–pathogen–month calendar with status, source, effective dates and reviewer; county eligibility additionally requires the historical catchment mapping. Unknown months remain missing. A documented continuous-program statement can support an explicit calendar assumption with listed exceptions; case presence cannot supply that statement.

In parallel, reconcile specimen-month totals to eligible annual records, resolve specimen-date versus surveillance-year differences, retain missing/imputed-date flags, and specify monthly exposure allocation from annual population. Diagnostic-method changes require a separate observation-model decision, even when surveillance stayed active. These requirements match [the combination protocol](extension_combination_protocol.md) and [seasonality readiness](extension_seasonality_readiness.md). No runtime files or eligibility calendars were changed by this review.
